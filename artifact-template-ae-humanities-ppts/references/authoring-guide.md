# Authoring and quality guide

Read this before creating or restyling a Humanities deck. The teacher's current instructions take priority.

## Consistent readable fonts

- Use **Georgia Bold** for deck titles and slide headings. Use **Arial Regular** for body text, quotations, instructions, captions, charts, tables, and notes. Use Arial Bold for short emphasis. Set the corresponding heading and body theme fonts when supported.
- No cursive, script, handwriting, calligraphy, ornamental, or narrowly condensed fonts. Keep sentences in normal capitalization. Convey the atmosphere through artwork, color, and conventional serif headings.
- Default sizes: deck title **52 pt**, slide heading **38 pt**, body **26 pt**, quotations **28–32 pt**, short labels **22 pt**. Aim for **24 pt or larger** for the material students must read. Table and diagram labels may use **20–22 pt** when clearly legible. Reserve **16–18 pt** for nonessential source credits and footer metadata.
- Split or simplify crowded content before shrinking text. Respect a fixed slide count by moving supporting detail into notes while retaining the quotations, exercises, and instructions students need. Do not silently remove required material.
- For Chinese text, use **Microsoft YaHei** for Chinese glyphs and retain Georgia/Arial for English. Confirm font availability in the rendering/export environment. If either English font is unavailable, choose one installed, readable serif for headings and one sans serif for body, use them consistently, and disclose the substitution.

## Visual identity and image reuse

Use deep umber `#211B16` and warm charcoal `#171512` as dark bases, warm ivory `#F2E8D5` for light text, parchment `#E9D8B6` for pale paper, muted brass `#B89B5E` for small accents, and burgundy `#5C2230` for occasional emphasis. Use ivory on dark surfaces and deep umber on parchment. Place teaching text on quiet surfaces with generous margins from candles, shelves, torn edges, seals, and frames.

Prefer the exact images in `assets/humanities-images/` over regenerating equivalents. Preserve transparency and aspect ratio, and keep overlaid text editable. Use full-slide backgrounds and paper panels selectively, with room for simpler text-and-artwork compositions. Art-studio imagery is available for relevant lessons. Use Hemingway and unsettling graphite images only when the lesson warrants them. AI illustrations of people, statues, and historical scenes are illustrative artwork, not documentary evidence.

## Varied layouts

Plan each slide's teaching purpose and layout family before authoring. Change composition, text location, artwork scale, column count, and reading direction across the sequence. Moving a wax seal or changing the background color alone does not create a new layout.

For a deck with ten or more slides, aim for at least six suitable layout families. Avoid three consecutive slides with the same composition unless a repeated exercise or comparison benefits from it. Short decks need only the layouts that serve their content. Balance explanation, source reading, visual observation, discussion, and student writing when the lesson supports those activities.

## Verify the deck

Render every slide and inspect text fit, font consistency, contrast over images, whitespace, and sequence-wide layout variety. Previews made without the real fonts can misreport fit; LibreOffice, for example, substitutes Georgia. On Windows with desktop PowerPoint, use [powerpoint-checks.md](powerpoint-checks.md) to render with the real fonts and measure overflow. Recheck affected slides after repairs. Keep required evidence, quotations, source images, and exercise instructions complete. Use appropriate notes for fact/image sources, including bundled artwork provenance. Verify actual motion in slideshow playback when available; static previews cannot prove click order or timing.
