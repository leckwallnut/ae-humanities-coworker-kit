# Simple Humanities lesson presets

Use these starting patterns when planning a new lesson or when the teacher asks for a preset. They guide the sequence and pacing; the teacher's material, learning goal, class level, requested slide count and time take priority. A restyling task should retain its existing lesson structure unless a change is requested.

The numbers in the Layout column refer to slides in [the editable layout library](../assets/teaching-layouts.pptx), not the final lesson's slide numbers. See the [layout index](layout-library.md) for text capacity and variants. Select only the layouts that serve the lesson; the library is a menu, not a requirement to use all 20 slides.

## Choose a starting pattern

| Preset | Best for | Suggested time | Starting slide count | Student outcome |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Brief explanation | One concept, distinction or writing move | 12 minutes | 4 | Apply the idea to a fresh example |
| Close reading | Meaning and technique in a supplied passage | 30 minutes | 6 | Support an interpretation with textual detail |
| Discussion | An open question with evidence to consider | 30 minutes | 5 | State, support and reconsider a position |
| Writing workshop | Producing and revising a short piece | 45 minutes | 7 | Finish a draft and make a purposeful revision |

Counts include every slide in the suggested sequence. Do not add a cover by default. A teacher's requested N slides means N total unless they explicitly ask for an additional cover. One slide may stay visible throughout an activity.

## Adapt before authoring

Use the teacher's stated topic, materials, learning goal, proficiency and available time. Infer what is reasonably clear from context. If time or level is unstated, choose a suitable starting pattern and note the assumptions briefly in the teacher notes. Ask only when missing information materially changes the lesson.

Choose one observable outcome, such as identifying an inference, supporting a reading with a quotation, or revising a sentence for a specific effect. Replace the library's sample passages, fictional profiles, tasks and word limits with content suited to that outcome. Preserve supplied assignment requirements and required homework/source images.

The minute allocations below cover instructions, thinking, reading, speaking, writing and transitions, not just teacher talk. For a different duration, protect the core student task and the final check; trim extra examples or whole-class reporting first. Do not simply speed up every stage. For longer or unfamiliar readings, allow more reading time and reduce other work.

For learners needing support, shorten instruction wording, pre-teach only essential vocabulary, allow quiet preparation and offer an optional sentence frame. For greater challenge, ask for a competing interpretation, qualification or independent transfer. Keep the intellectual purpose while adjusting language support.

In the first slide's notes, identify the selected preset, total planned minutes, learning outcome and any assumptions. In each slide's notes, record its time, teacher action, student action and useful answer or discussion cues. Keep timing on slides only when it helps students complete an activity. Remove the library's template labels and capacity guides from classroom-facing slides.

## Brief explanation

**Starting point:** 12 minutes, 4 slides. Explain one concept and check whether students can use it. Suitable material includes a literary term, an analytical distinction or one writing technique.

| Lesson slide | Stage | Minutes | Teacher and student activity | Layout |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Notice a problem | 2 | Show a short example and ask what students notice. Collect a few initial ideas. | 02: focal quotation |
| 2 | Explain the concept | 3 | Give a plain definition and connect it to the example. Keep new terminology limited. | 05: one term in depth |
| 3 | Work through an example | 4 | Model the reasoning or revision. Students identify which detail makes it work. | 15: worked revision, or 08 for a conceptual comparison |
| 4 | Apply and check | 3 | Give a fresh example. Students respond independently, compare briefly and explain their choice. | 16: model then transfer |

**If time changes:** For 8 minutes, use 1 + 2 + 3 + 2 minutes. With more time, add a second application or a student explanation rather than more definitions. If students struggle, return to one concrete example and keep the final application small.

**Adaptation:** For a sequence or historical process, replace slide 2 with layout 13. Change the comparison or model to match the concept rather than forcing a sentence-revision task.

**Prompt example:** Use the AE Humanities PPTs skill (artifact-template-ae-humanities-ppts) with the Brief explanation preset. Make a 12-minute lesson on the difference between observation and inference for intermediate English learners, using my examples.

## Close reading

**Starting point:** 30 minutes, 6 slides. Use a supplied passage and one interpretive question. A longer reading can remain in a handout while slides enlarge the exact excerpts needed for analysis.

| Lesson slide | Stage | Minutes | Teacher and student activity | Layout |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Set the reading question | 2 | Establish the purpose and only the context needed to begin. Invite an initial response. | 02: focal quotation |
| 2 | Remove a reading barrier | 3 | Clarify up to three essential words or one piece of context. Let students try a meaning in context. | 06: terms in context |
| 3 | Read for the situation | 5 | Students read quietly, identify what happens and check basic understanding with a partner. | 01: full passage |
| 4 | Read for details | 7 | Model one observation. Pairs annotate two further details and explain their possible effects. | 03: margin notes |
| 5 | Compare interpretations | 8 | Groups compare supported readings and test a competing explanation. Return to exact wording. | 04: evidence and comment |
| 6 | Write an exit response | 5 | Each student states an interpretation, quotes or identifies a detail and explains the connection. | 16: model then transfer |

**If time changes:** For 20 minutes, use 2 + 2 + 4 + 5 + 4 + 3 minutes with a shorter passage. With more time, add a rereading or a second passage using layout 07. If comprehension is difficult, reduce the number of details studied before cutting reading time.

**Adaptation:** Skip unnecessary vocabulary teaching; use that stage for reading or context instead. Biography layouts 11 or 12 are useful only when verified biographical information helps the chosen reading question. Do not invent a biography or replace the teacher's passage with the library's fiction.

**Prompt example:** Use the AE Humanities PPTs skill (artifact-template-ae-humanities-ppts) with the Close reading preset. Build a 30-minute lesson around this passage. Students should explain how two details create the mood. Include possible readings in the notes.

## Discussion

**Starting point:** 30 minutes, 5 slides. Use a question that permits reasoned responses and give learners shared evidence before they speak.

| Lesson slide | Stage | Minutes | Teacher and student activity | Layout |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Frame the question | 3 | Clarify the question and key terms. Allow an initial view without requiring an immediate public answer. | 09: claim and counterpoint |
| 2 | Examine evidence | 5 | Students read a short source or compare two excerpts. Identify evidence that may support different positions. | 01: full passage, or 07: paired passages |
| 3 | Prepare a contribution | 4 | Students write a claim, supporting detail and question. Offer a speaking frame if useful. | 16: model then transfer |
| 4 | Discuss and respond | 13 | Use small-group roles, listen for evidence and invite a response to a different reading. Include time to rotate roles. | 10: discussion roles |
| 5 | Reconsider and conclude | 5 | Students record their current view, the evidence behind it and one unresolved question. Sample a few responses. | 08: comparison matrix adapted for synthesis |

**If time changes:** For 20 minutes, use 2 + 4 + 3 + 7 + 4 minutes. With more time, add a second round in which students respond to an argument they heard. If discussion stalls, provide quiet preparation and return to a specific source detail.

**Adaptation:** The layout's FOR/AGAINST labels are optional. Use qualified positions or several interpretations when appropriate. Do not force every question into a binary debate or invent evidence to make opposing positions appear equally supported. The conclusion can identify uncertainty rather than consensus.

**Prompt example:** Use the AE Humanities PPTs skill (artifact-template-ae-humanities-ppts) with the Discussion preset. Make a 30-minute lesson about preserving historic buildings, using these two readings as evidence. Include preparation time and roles.

## Writing workshop

**Starting point:** 45 minutes, 7 slides. Help students plan, draft, receive focused feedback and revise. Choose a manageable product from the actual assignment, such as a paragraph, scene or short response.

| Lesson slide | Stage | Minutes | Teacher and student activity | Layout |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Understand the task | 4 | Explain the audience, purpose, product and two or three success criteria. Check that the task is clear. | 17: assignment brief |
| 2 | Examine a model | 5 | Compare a draft and revision. Students explain how one change serves the writing goal. | 15: worked revision |
| 3 | Try the writing move | 5 | Students complete a small parallel example before writing independently. Discuss a few possibilities. | 16: model then transfer |
| 4 | Make a plan | 4 | Students choose their main idea, sequence or evidence. Keep the plan brief and usable. | 18: timed workshop adapted to planning |
| 5 | Draft independently | 15 | Keep the prompt and success criteria visible. Allow sustained writing and brief individual support. | 17: assignment brief reused |
| 6 | Exchange focused feedback | 7 | Pairs identify a strength and one useful revision. Use a checklist or a short conference. | 19: evidence checklist, or 20: conference |
| 7 | Revise and reflect | 5 | Writers make one purposeful change and briefly explain its effect. | 18: timed workshop adapted to revision |

**If time changes:** For 30 minutes, use 3 + 4 + 3 + 3 + 10 + 4 + 3 minutes and choose a smaller writing product. In the four-minute feedback stage, use one brief written comment per writer rather than two full conferences. With more time, protect an additional drafting/revision block.

**Adaptation:** For the default seven-minute feedback stage, two three-minute conferences plus one minute to swap roles fit the plan. Replace sample times in layout 18 and the example word range in layout 17; neither is a fixed requirement. For an essay or long assignment, focus the workshop on one section rather than promising a complete essay in fifteen minutes. Feedback criteria should match the taught writing move.

**Prompt example:** Use the AE Humanities PPTs skill (artifact-template-ae-humanities-ppts) with the Writing workshop preset. Plan a 45-minute lesson in which students write a short atmospheric scene. Use a model, drafting time and focused peer feedback. Adapt the length for intermediate learners.

## Check the lesson plan

Confirm that stage minutes sum to the stated duration, the final slide count matches the teacher's request, and the sequence includes time for the intended student outcome. Check that any reused layout's old title, timing, word range, sample answer and fictional details have been replaced as needed. A plan is adaptable: record useful adjustments in teacher notes without presenting the suggested timing as a guarantee.
