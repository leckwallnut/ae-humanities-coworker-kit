# Dark academia design system

The user described the desired atmosphere in **AE Humanities ppts** as dim candles, dark libraries, wax seals, and ripped paper. Keep it scholarly and tactile, with readable conventional type and compositions that support teaching. The teaching layout library shows the palette and the treatment of classroom material.

## Typography and spacing

| Role | Font | Default size | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deck title | Georgia Bold | 52 pt | Short, generous space |
| Slide heading | Georgia Bold | 38 pt | Usually one or two lines |
| Body and task instructions | Arial Regular | 26 pt | Left aligned, normal capitalization |
| Quotation | Arial Regular | 28–32 pt | Exact wording, roman type |
| Author/source attribution | Arial Regular | 22 pt | Separate from the quotation |
| Labels and brief steps | Arial Regular/Bold | 22 pt | Clear hierarchy |
| Dense editable table labels | Arial Regular | 20–22 pt | Only when comfortably readable |
| Nonessential footer/source metadata | Arial Regular | 16–18 pt | Never the lesson's key instruction |

Use the same font roles throughout the deck, including native charts and tables. Avoid adding a typewriter font for a typewriter image. Use bold instead of cursive or decorative italics for emphasis. Preserve italics that have a necessary semantic role, such as a work's title, while keeping long teaching passages in roman type.

Start body leading around 1.15–1.3 times the type size when supported. Leave at least a visible line of breathing room between distinct ideas. Keep outer margins roughly 5–7% of slide width and height. Within textured paper, measure the visible paper interior rather than the PNG's outer rectangle; transparent margins, curls, flowers, seals, and borders consume space.

Keep long reading passages on quiet parchment or a solid dark surface. Split at meaningful paragraph boundaries where the requested slide count allows it. Use enlarged excerpts for close analysis rather than shrinking an entire worksheet. If a supplied scan contains small or decorative text that must be retained, keep the evidence and add readable editable excerpts for the class.

## Palette

| Color | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deep umber | `#211B16` | Main background and text on pale paper |
| Warm charcoal | `#171512` | Quiet dark surface |
| Warm ivory | `#F2E8D5` | Text on dark images/backgrounds |
| Parchment | `#E9D8B6` | Pale paper and occasional light slide |
| Muted brass | `#B89B5E` | Restrained accents, rules, small ornaments |
| Burgundy | `#5C2230` | Seals, section accents, limited emphasis |
| Muted olive | `#6B7258` | Optional botanical accent |

Ivory on deep umber and deep umber on parchment are the principal text pairings. Assess contrast against the actual image behind each text box. Prefer moving text to a quieter region or changing the background to forcing text across shelving or a candle's glow. A subtle supported dark overlay may help, but do not obscure the source artwork unnecessarily. Brass and olive are primarily accents; they should not carry long passages over busy images.

## Composition

Use one clear visual focus per slide. A bust beside a short argument, a large parchment quotation, or a library wall with text can each carry the atmosphere. Leave quiet space between ornament and teaching content. A few carefully chosen accents are enough; avoid decorating every corner.

Whole-slide backgrounds are useful for titles, short explanations, discussion prompts, and synthesis. Pale paper supports close reading and student writing tasks. Alternate those surfaces where the lesson benefits from a visual reset. Keep charts, required tables, and requested diagrams native and editable. Use the supplied images for decorative artwork.

Default to widescreen 16:9 for a new deck, matching the bundled library backgrounds. Preserve an existing deck's dimensions during restyling unless the user requests conversion. Never stretch artwork to fill a different ratio. Preview cropping because it can remove the image's intended quiet text space.
