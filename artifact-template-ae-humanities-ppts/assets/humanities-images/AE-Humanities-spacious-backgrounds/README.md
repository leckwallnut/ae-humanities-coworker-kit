# AE Humanities: backgrounds with generous text space

Twenty-four separate, text-free widescreen images (high-quality JPEG copies): six settings, each with space for text on the left, right, center or across a wide area. These complement the six original library backgrounds and the twelve quiet paper surfaces already in the Humanities skill.

If you have the coworker kit, start with the visual background guide at `Extras/Visual guides/Spacious backgrounds - Visual background guide.pdf`; it is not installed with the skill. Its six pages show all 24 images with sample text and suggested reading areas. The dotted boxes and words are only in the guide; the images are blank. The guide's type sizes refer to a 13.33 x 7.5 inch widescreen slide, not the size of its reduced preview cards.

## Choose a setting and composition

| Setting | Asset IDs | Possible teaching uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading rooms | 94-97 | Reading, reflection, academic inquiry |
| Archives | 98-101 | Memory, evidence, records and research |
| Museum galleries | 102-105 | Interpretation, cultural value and identity |
| Cloisters | 106-109 | Reflection, journeys and ethical choices |
| Theatres | 110-113 | Dialogue, roles, performance and debate |
| Rainy studies | 114-117 | Mood, inference and narrative writing |

In every group, the order is left, right, center, wide. Filenames identify both setting and text position. These are optional associations; let the lesson's material determine the choice.

- **Left or right:** a brief explanation, vocabulary focus, discussion prompt or instructions beside a visible setting.
- **Center:** a focused question, quotation or short passage framed by architecture.
- **Wide:** a longer passage, an explanation with one example, or two brief comparison columns.

Start with **30-45 body words** for side/center compositions and **50-65 body words** for wide compositions, plus one short heading. The guide shows a 33-word passage in side/center examples and a 48-word passage in wide examples. These are starting ranges, not fixed capacities. Long words, bullets, two columns or a two-line heading use more room. Split crowded content into another slide; use the quiet paper collection for longer close-reading passages.

## Put teaching text on the background

1. Use a 16:9 slide, ideally 13.33 x 7.5 inches. Place the image as a full-slide background, preserving its proportions. Every source is 1672 x 941 pixels, within one pixel of exact 16:9. Avoid cropping into the quiet reading area.
2. Add the heading and body as separate editable text boxes. Use **Georgia Bold 38 pt** for a short heading and **Arial 26 pt** for body text, with about **1.25 line spacing**. Keep student reading text at least 24 pt. No cursive.
3. Use warm ivory **#F2E8D5** on reading-room, museum, theatre and rainy-study scenes. Use deep umber **#211B16** on pale archive and cloister scenes. Choose a plain solid text color; shadows and outlines are unnecessary in the suggested areas.
4. Use the per-image coordinates below as a starting area. Text boxes with internal margins should fit inside this area; do not let an automatic text-fit setting silently shrink the font. Keep paragraph text left aligned, including within a centered composition.
5. Check the finished slides at presentation size after export. Keep text clear of shelves, lamps, curtains, statues and architectural edges. Change composition when the teaching purpose changes; alternate scene backgrounds with quieter reading slides.

The guide samples use the exact fonts above. Programmatic checks verified sample wrapping, opaque images, unique image hashes and contrast throughout the suggested reading rectangles. All six PDF pages were visually reviewed. These checks do not establish projector performance or PowerPoint export behavior; verify the finished classroom deck separately.

## Provenance and reuse

Created with the built-in image_gen tool on 27 October 2026, one opaque background per image. These are invented illustrative settings; the paintings, statues and architecture are not documentary sources. The original PNGs are kept unchanged in the coworker kit's `Extras/Original images` folder. The skill uses JPEG copies. Exact generation prompts are included in **README-prompts.md**. A suitable speaker-note credit is: "AI-generated AE Humanities background, [filename]."

The skill contains JPEG copies of all 24 backgrounds, this usage guide and the catalog. The original PNGs and the visual guide are in the coworker kit's `Extras` folder. The installed Humanities skill routes to this collection automatically when it suits a new lesson; the collection does not need to be regenerated.
