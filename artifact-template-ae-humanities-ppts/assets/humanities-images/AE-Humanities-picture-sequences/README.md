## A simple 10-minute routine

Adapt timing and writing length to the class. Try one sequence at a time.

1. **Observe - 1 minute.** Show each image large enough to inspect. Ask students to list only what they can see: "The chair is empty" is an observation; "The student feels lonely" is an interpretation.
2. **Infer - 3 minutes.** Compare two or three frames. Ask what changed, what could have caused it, and which clue supports the claim. Invite at least two plausible explanations.
3. **Write - 5 minutes.** Show A and C while withholding B. Write the missing scene using the set's task below. For prose, 80-120 words is a flexible starting point; a simpler option is four to six meaningful sentences.
4. **Share - 1 minute.** A partner points out two clues used well and one place where cause or time is unclear. Reveal B afterward as one visual possibility, not as the only correct story.

For a brief discussion, stop after inference. For a longer workshop, develop viewpoint, dialogue or a different ending. Students may suggest another order if they can explain the evidence and make the sequence coherent.

Useful language: "I can see ..." / "Between A and C, ... changes." / "This might suggest ... because ..." / "Another explanation is ..." / "We cannot tell whether ..."

## Sequence prompts

### At the station

**Visible clues**

- A: A woman wears a burgundy scarf; a suitcase rests on the bench.
- B: She carries the suitcase away; the scarf lies on the bench.
- C: The woman and case are gone; the scarf is folded beneath a white flower.

**Observe:** Track the woman, the suitcase and the scarf. Which detail changes without anyone being shown changing it?

**Infer:** Who might have folded the scarf and added the flower? Offer two explanations and support each with a visible clue.

**Write:** Hide B. Write 80-120 words linking A to C. Give the scarf a meaning without directly explaining the emotion.

**Another route:** For a different gap, hide C and write two endings: one reassuring and one unsettling.

**Language support:** left behind, folded, returned, might have, perhaps.

**Leave open:** We can see the changed scarf, but we cannot know who folded it or why the flower is there.

### Behind the gate

**Visible clues**

- A: The gate is chained shut; a drooping sapling stands in a pot outside.
- B: The gate is open; a gardener waters the potted sapling.
- C: The sapling is planted inside; the empty pot and watering can remain outside.

**Observe:** Compare the gate, the chain, the pot and the plant. List what is visibly different before explaining why.

**Infer:** What actions could connect these moments? Who might have opened the garden, and what evidence supports your explanation?

**Write:** Hide B. Write 80-120 words linking A to C. Show care through two concrete actions and include a plausible passage of time.

**Another route:** For a shorter task, write three cause-and-effect sentences using because, so and as a result.

**Language support:** unlocked, watered, transplanted, because, as a result.

**Leave open:** The gardener is visible in B. Their identity, ownership of the garden and reasons for helping are not established.

### Room at the table

**Visible clues**

- A: One student sits with a closed burgundy book; the opposite chair is empty.
- B: A second student stands beside that chair with an open notebook.
- C: Both sit together and attend to the now-open burgundy book.

**Observe:** Compare the empty chair, the book and the students' body positions. What is visible, and what are you only guessing?

**Infer:** What might have been said between B and C? Give two plausible exchanges. Avoid treating an expression as proof of a feeling.

**Write:** Hide B. Write a short scene linking A to C with four to six lines of dialogue and two small physical actions.

**Another route:** Retell the same moment from the other student's viewpoint. Keep the visible facts the same.

**Language support:** hesitated, asked, offered, leaned forward, seemed.

**Leave open:** Shared attention is visible in C. Whether the students are friends, strangers or classmates is left open.

### The lantern

**Visible clues**

- A: The door is closed, one upstairs window glows, and the hanging lantern is unlit.
- B: The door is open, the window is dark, and the lantern stands by the doorway.
- C: The door and windows are dark; the hanging lantern is lit and shoeprints mark the path.

**Observe:** Track the door, the upstairs window and the lantern. What new trace is visible on the path in C?

**Infer:** Who could have moved and lit the lantern? Offer an ordinary explanation and an unsettling one, using the same clues.

**Write:** Hide B. Write 80-120 words linking A to C. Use clear, concrete sentences and leave one question unanswered.

**Another route:** Alternatively, show A and B only. Predict the next image, then compare your ending with C.

**Language support:** ajar, unlit, footprints, must have, could have.

**Leave open:** No person is shown. The prints do not establish who made them, when they were made, or the direction of travel.

## What successful work does

A successful response keeps the visible facts consistent, uses at least two specific clues, connects events with a plausible cause or time sequence, and uses clear sentences. Students can invent motives, relationships and missing actions; these should be compatible with the pictures and identified as interpretation. There is no required hidden solution. Comment on evidence and coherence rather than agreement with a teacher's preferred plot.

## Use in PowerPoint

Insert each image separately, preserve its aspect ratio, and use a 16:9 slide. The 1672 x 941 source files are within one pixel of exact 16:9. Start with one image per slide, then show a three-image comparison after students know the details. Keep the entire bench, gate, table or house/path visible; tight crops can remove a narrative clue. A three-image strip is an overview and may be too small for first viewing.

Put instructions in separate editable text boxes on a quiet paper or solid background. Keep Georgia Bold headings and Arial body text, normally 38 pt and 26 pt. The scenes are not designed as backgrounds for substantial reading text. Use plain A/B/C labels only when helpful; the images themselves contain no captions or sequence labels. A static sequence of slides is sufficient. If adding a native reveal effect, follow the existing motion-pilot guide and verify the final PPTX separately.

The intended clues are recorded in **asset-catalog.json**, alongside grouping, order, dimensions, alt text and hashes. Small variations in texture or brushwork are not intended puzzle clues. Use a verbal description when a student needs access to a visual detail. Actual classroom projection may need a full-image view, especially for the lantern on the doorstep.

These are invented AI-generated illustrations, not photographs of real people, historical events or places. A short speaker-note credit is sufficient: "AI-generated AE Humanities picture sequence, [filename]." Exact prompts and continuity reference filenames are in **README-prompts.md**.
