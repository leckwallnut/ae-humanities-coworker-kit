# Teaching layouts

For actual slides to copy, see the [20-slide layout index](layout-library.md) and [editable teaching layout library](../assets/teaching-layouts.pptx). For a lesson sequence and suggested pacing, choose a pattern from [lesson-presets.md](lesson-presets.md). The layout library is a menu; a lesson need not use every layout.

Choose layouts by the lesson's needs. These are a repertoire, not a compulsory deck sequence. Sketch the slide's teaching purpose, composition, image, text region, and any reveal before authoring. Review the sequence as a montage: changes should be visible in the placement and scale of content, not only in decoration.

The six library backgrounds provide useful placements, but six positions on the same type of photograph do not by themselves supply all the teaching variety. Combine these with close reading, image study, biography, comparison, and writing layouts.

Use the open-book and paired-paper assets for short comparisons. Their folds, decorative margins, and narrow pages limit text capacity. For longer comparisons, use wider open columns on a quiet surface. Divider strips and wax-seal labels suit short headings, not paragraphs.

A sequence might alternate library opener, image observation, close reading, vocabulary, discussion, comparison, writing, and synthesis. Retain the user's lesson order and exercise count. Do not insert a section break or activity just to reach a layout quota.

For progressive reveals, retain the question or passage while revealing the answer or interpretation as a separate object. Background photographs remain visible. Use complete ideas rather than individual word animation.
